0 — TL;DR: Why dLocal is not Adyen

The bull/bear split in one minute

dLocal is a single-API router for getting paid and paying out in the hardest 40 markets on earth. Where Adyen consolidates developed-market acquiring (cards + Apple Pay), dLocal consolidates local payment-method chaos in Brazil (PIX/Boleto), Mexico (OXXO/SPEI), Nigeria (bank transfers + USSD), India (UPI), Egypt (Fawry), Turkey (installments), South Africa, Argentina, etc. — 900+ methods across ~40 countries. Global merchants plug once (“One dLocal”) instead of building 40 local entities and banking relationships.

1 — History: Uruguay’s first unicorn, built from a payments spin-out

2016–2026: from AstroPay garage to $1B revenue

2016: The spin-out. Sergio Fogel (INSEAD MBA, ex-IBM Haifa researcher, Oracle LATAM, founder of Uniotel VoIP and ElAgora B2B) and Andrés Bzurovski build AstroPay, a prepaid-card processor for EM e-commerce where cards fail. Sebastián Kanovich, 30-year-old CEO, sees the bigger wedge: global merchants (Booking, Uber, Netflix) need one integration, not 30. dLocal spins out of AstroPay in Jan 2016 in Montevideo — Uruguay, population 3.4m, no tech tradition — with the pitch “let merchants sell like locals without opening local entities.” Uruguay’s first tech company to matter since… ever.

2016–2020: Product before press. dLocal builds “One dLocal” — single API with 600+ methods by 2020, handling pay-ins (local cards, bank transfers, cash vouchers, wallets) and pay-outs (seller settlements, refunds, payouts). Capital-light: floats on merchant funds, no consumer balance sheet. Backed by General Atlantic ($200m at $1.2B in Sep 2020 — the moment it becomes Uruguay’s first unicorn). Revenue ~$80m (2019) → $237m (2021), EBITDA positive from day one. The pandemic (e-commerce + streaming surge in LATAM) is the accelerant.

June 2021: IPO. Nasdaq: DLO at $21, opens $32, peaks $73 intraday Sep 2021 — $15B+ mkt cap on 294m diluted shares. The prospectus hammers the same line: “technology-first payments platform tailored for emerging markets.” CIA: Cayman/Malta? Actually Malta holding with Uruguay operating co. Reports in USD/IFRS. Lock-ups: Fogel/Bzurovski retain ~45% vote via Class B.

2022–2023: The short attack + credibility test. Muddy Waters (Nov 2022) alleges accounting irregularities, related-party opacity (AstroPay ties), and inflated TPV. Stock halves to $11. dLocal responds with audit committee review (PwC), restates segment disclosure, replaces Kanovich as CEO (returns as EVP Tech) with Pedro Arnt (ex-COO MercadoLibre, ex-CFO ML) as CEO from 2023, and adds Big Four + new independent directors. The business keeps growing (TPV $17.2B ’22 → $28.9B ’23), which helps — but Muddy never fully retracts, and the discount persists.

2024: The FX year. Q1–Q2 2024 exposes the model’s FX sensitivity: Argentina pesos -56% QoQ, Nigeria naira -70% YoY, Egypt pound -40%. TPV still +41% YoY, but revenue +34% and take rate drops to 2.59% (from 3.08%) as high-spread FX corridors collapse and mix shifts to lower-margin Brazil/Mexico volume. NRR 100–110% ex-Nigeria 114%. Stock trades $7–9 — value vs volatility debate hardens. dLocal adds AI/automation, cuts costs, pushes geographic diversification (Africa + Asia ex-LATAM rises to ~35% of TPV by late 2024).

2 — How dLocal actually works

One API, two flows, 900 local methods — and where the take rate comes from

The plumbing: pay-in + pay-out

Pay-in (collect): A U.S. SaaS wants to sell in Brazil in 12× installments on local Visa, in MXN via OXXO cash, in INR via UPI. dLocal is Merchant of Record — licensed locally (or via bank partner) — processes in local currency, handles installments, fraud, reconciliation, then settles to the merchant in USD/EUR (or local) via one API. Merchant avoids 40 entities, 40 bank accounts, 40 compliance teams.

Pay-out (disburse): A marketplace (Booking, Uber, Temu) needs to pay local sellers/drivers in BRL, MXN, NGN, EGP, ZAR, TRY. dLocal does mass pay-outs via local bank rails (PIX, SPEI, EFT) — often the same rails as pay-ins, but reversed. This is the wedge Adyen can’t match cheaply: two-way money movement in one contract.

The float, but not the Berkshire float: dLocal holds merchant funds briefly (settlement cycle 1–7 days) — not insurance float. Net cash on BS ~$1.0B (from merchant balances) earns overnight interest, but it’s working capital, not investable forever. Value is in the routing fee, not the balance.

Product split (where mix matters)

  • Pay-in ~68% of TPV (Q1’26) — higher take rate (local cards ~3.0–3.5%, APMs ~2.0–2.5%); cross-border pay-ins (settled in USD) earn FX spread.
  • Pay-out ~32% of TPV — lower take rate (~0.8–1.2%) but sticky (marketplace payouts are mission-critical). Growth here compresses blended take rate but grows absolute GP dollars — the 2024–2025 story.

How dLocal makes money

Revenue = TPV × take rate (net). Take rate = processing fee + FX spread + installments markup, net of interchange/bank costs passed through. Gross profit = revenue − direct processing costs (interchange, acquirer fees, bank commissions). So: GP margin = GP / Revenue (36–40% recently, was 65% in 2019 — mix shift story).

What each KPI tells you

  • TPV: The volume truth — grows even when FX crushes revenue. 6 straight quarters 50%+ (Q4’24 59% → Q1’26 73%) is the rerating driver. Pay-in vs pay-out split (~68/32 in Q1’26) explains take-rate drift.

4 — Moats, Competition & Risks

Why 900 methods aren’t 900 moats — and what could unwind it

3 moat sources (and how durable)

1) Regulatory + banking graph in 40 hard markets. Each country is a mini-fintech charter: licenses (or bank sponsorship), tax handling (Brazil IOF, Argentina retentions), settlement rails (PIX, SPEI, UPI, MPesa), and KYC. dLocal has spent 8 yrs accumulating them. Stripe/Adyen have 3–5 of these; dLocal has 40. Durability: high for pay-outs, medium for pay-ins — PIX/SPEI are open rails, but the settlement + FX + compliance bundle is still needed.

2) Two-way network (pay-in + pay-out). Amazon pays sellers in BRL via dLocal *and* collects from buyers via dLocal — one reconciliation, one FX desk. Pure acquirers (Adyen) don’t do mass pay-outs cheaply; pure remittance (Wise) doesn’t do local acquiring. This bundling is the contracting moat — switching means rebuilding *both* flows.

3) Emerging-market data moat (fraud/approval). 8 yrs of transaction data in high-fraud corridors (Nigeria, Egypt) improves approval rates 200–400bps vs generic acquirer — directly lifts merchant revenue, which is why NRR stays >106% even when FX crashes.

Risks that break the thesis

  • FX + take-rate double hit: 2024 Nigeria/Argentina/Egypt devaluations cut NRR to 100% and GP margin to 37.6% — TPV growth masked it. A synchronized EM devaluation + Brazil PIX fee cap could push blended take → 2.0% and revenue +15% not +40%.
  • Concentration: Top merchant ~10–15% of revenue, top 10 ~55–60% (Spotify, Amazon, Netflix, Temu historically). Losing one = -10% rev + NRR dip. Temu/Shein churn risk as they build local entities.
  • Regulatory: Brazil BCB, Mexico CNBV, India RBI, Nigeria CBN can change cross-border settlement, FX retention, or licensing overnight (India PPI license was a prior bottleneck). Single-API = single point of regulatory failure.
  • MercadoLibre-like verticalization: If big EM e-commerce builds own local rails (ML, Naspers), pay-out demand shrinks. dLocal’s non-Mercado growth (Africa + Asia ex-LATAM → 35% of TPV) is the hedge — watch its share.

What would I need to believe to NOT own it? That take rate converges to ~2.0% by 2028 and TPV growth slows to ~25% (merchant concentration + competition) — then revenue CAGR ~20%, GP margin 32–34%, EBITDA/GP stalls at 65%, and $14.76 at 22× earnings is 12× EBITDA on $400m — fair, not cheap. The bull needs take rate to stabilize at 2.4–2.6% while TPV stays 35–45% — that’s the 2025→Q1’26 evidence.

Reverse DCF — what $14.76 implies

At EV $3.4B, with 1% dilution/yr and no debt, $14.76 implies ~10.5% unlevered IRR for 5 yrs if FCF (net income + SBC − capex ~8m) grows 22% CAGR and you exit at 15× FCF in 2031 (≈ $530m FCF in ’28 → ~$850m in ’31). Put differently: market expects revenue ~$1.5B in ’26 (we do) then deceleration to ~15% by ’30 — not re-acceleration. If you think TPV stays 40%+ at 2.4% take, 22% FCF CAGR is low and IRR is ~14–16% to a 18× exit. If you think take → 2.0% and TPV → 20%, IRR is ~6–7%.

What would make me not own it

I would not own dLocal if I believed (1) local regulators close the cross-border settlement window (Brazil BCB requires local entity; India RBI could block payout flows), (2) take rate goes to 2.0% by 2027 without TPV re-accelerating past 50% (pure price competition from Adyen/Nuvei/local banks), or (3) the “One dLocal” moat is really a banking-relationship roll-up that a better-funded competitor replicates in 18 months.
