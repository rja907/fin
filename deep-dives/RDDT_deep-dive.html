1 — History: The 19-Year Overnight Success

Reddit survived three owners, a decade of intentional neglect, and a user revolt — which is why the data moat exists.

2005 — Two UVA roommates. Steve Huffman & Alexis Ohanian (Y Combinator W05) built "the front page of the internet" — a link aggregator where anyone could create a community (subreddit). Paul Graham's coldest email: "change the title."

2006-11 — Condé Nast exile. Sold to Condé Nast for $10-20m 14 months after launch. Reddit languished as a magazine side-project, kept alive by volunteers (mods) while Digg died of its own redesign. The neglect was the gift: unpaid mods built 100k+ communities, moderation norms, and karma — a corpus no competitor replicated.

2011 — Spin, near-death. Spun out with Advance ownership. Traffic grew, revenue didn't: 2014 revenue ~$8m, 2015 Huffman returned as CEO after Ellen Pao's crisis (content policy + AMA revolt). Huffman focused on product (apps) and monetization (ads), but advertisers saw Reddit as brand-unsafe (unmoderated porn/hate).

2016-22 — The ad machine. Jen Wong (ex-Time, PopSugar) joined as COO 2018, built ads: managed service → auction. DAUq 52m (2019) → 73m (2021). Revenue $230m (2020) → $804m (2023). Still loss-making, SBC-heavy, and dependent on Google traffic (40%+). Fate changed when LLMs needed data.

2023-24 — The LLM windfall. Sam Altman (OpenAI) was an early investor (~8.7% pre-IPO). In early 2024 Reddit signed $60m/yr Google (Training API + Vertex) and $70m OpenAI data-licensing deals — suddenly "other revenue" (licensing) went from $13m (2022) to $84m (2024) to $120m+ run-rate. IPO March 21, 2024 at $34 ($6.4bn FD). First-day close $50.44 — retail meme love, but short report on Google dependence.

2025-26 — Profit inflection. 2024 revenue $1,300m (+61.7% YoY), still GAAP loss −$484m (IPO SBC burst $350m). 2025 revenue $2,203m (+69.4% YoY), GAAP net +$530m (14.2% margin) — first profitable year, driven by ads + licensing leverage. Q2 2026 (Jul 30): revenue $805m (+61.1% YoY) vs $744m est (+8.2% beat), ad +64% to $762m, other +24% to $43m, DAUq ~113m (est), EBITDA ~$160m, OCF $262m (TTM >$1bn first time), buyback $235m at $157.57 avg. Guidance raised and stock +7% after-hours to $161.70.

Why history matters: Reddit's corpus is 2.2bn posts + 17bn comments in 190 languages — impossible to recreate because each subreddit is a human-labeled training set. That + volunteer mods is why Google/OpenAI pay.

1. Advertising — the engine (95%)

Inventory: Feed (in-feed native), Conversation (inside comment threads — highest intent), Search (top of results — Google's nightmare). Advertisers target by interest (r/wallstreetbets, r/SkincareAddiction), not friend graph — so signal is contextual, not personal. CAPI (Conversions API) lets Shopify see ROAS.

New 2024-26: Reddit Pro (businesses manage presence), Dynamic Product Ads (catalog feed → thread), and "Ask" (Reddit Answers) — AI answering with citations inside Reddit. Q2 2026 management: performance ads now >40% of ad revenue (was brand-heavy).

ARPU $7.12 Q2 (global) = $6.70 US, $2.9 Intl — vs Meta $13.50, Pinterest $6.40, Snap $3.30. Gap is the opportunity.

2. Data licensing — the high-margin kicker (5% but 100% flow-through)

Deals: Google (60m/yr + expanded to Search: Reddit posts surface in Google SGE with attribution), OpenAI (GPT training + real-time API), plus experimental Sprinklr/Cision. No chunk is exclusive — Reddit can sell same data multiple times. Q2 other revenue $43m (+24% YoY) — management said renewals are Annual Recurring, not one-off. Margin: 90%+.

Why not bigger? Reddit caps at ~$150m/yr intentionally — over-licensing would let LLMs replace Reddit. Strategy: license enough to fund mods, keep traffic.

3. The plumbing — logged-in vs logged-out

Logged-in DAUq (~52m) — 6.2 min/day, 3× ARPU vs logged-out; they post, vote, give signal. Logged-out (~61m) — arrive via Google, read one post, leave; 1.8× higher volume but 70% bounce. Strategy: convert logged-out → logged-in via "Open in app" + Google licensing that forces attribution (growth 2025 WAU 444m → DAUq 113m implies conversion improving). WAU/DAU ratio: 444m WAU (Q3 2025 disclosed) / 113m DAU ≈ 3.9× — similar to Snap 3.5×, lower than Meta 1.8× (Reddit's daily habit weaker).

4 — Competitive Dynamics & Moats

Moats (durable → fragile)

1. Corpus / labeled conversations

Every subreddit is a labeled dataset ("Should I buy X?") that Meta/X don't have. LLMs pay because human preference data + vote ranking is RLHF gold. Durability: medium — Reddit can poison via licensing terms; but if users stop posting (AI spam), corpus decays. Backstop: mods ban AI slop.

2. Volunteer mods + subreddit brand

60k mods enforce norms for free (~$3.4m value per MIT). No competitor can hire that. But 2023 API protest showed fragility — mods blacked out Reddit for 2 days. Trust is moat only if Huffman doesn't break it (he did once).

3. Interest graph vs social graph

Pinterest has it; Meta doesn't. Advertiser can target r/BabyBumps without knowing who you are — privacy-safe. That's why auto/CPG love Reddit. Limit: logged-out growth is Google-dependent.

4. Search + Google symbiosis

40% traffic from Google ("reddit + query" hack). Google pays $60m/yr to keep it, because Reddit answers are better than Gemini. Risk: Google now surfaces Reddit in AI Overviews with attribution — traffic could fall 20% if Overviews answer inside Google.

5 — Risks

1. Google dependence & AI Overviews

~40% Sessions from Google. If Google answers query with AI Overview that scrapes Reddit (even with license), clicks drop. Renaissance: Reddit Answers cannibalizes its own search — needs to convert searcher to logged-in.

2. Community trust collapse (the 2023 lesson)

API pricing revolt closed 8k subreddits. Mods are unpaid — over-monetization (more ads in conversation) risks exodus. Huffman apologized; but trust is thin.

3. Licensing ceiling

Other revenue $143m (6.5% rev) in 2025 — growing only 24% in Q2 while ads +64%. If $60m Google + $70m OpenAI are capped, licensing doesn't scale to $300m as bulls hope. Renewal 2027 is binary.

4. SBC & profitability fragility

2024 net −$484m due to SBC $548m (42% rev). 2025 net +$530m only because SBC fell to 23% — leverage. Q1-Q2 2026 SBC still ~20% — GAAP earnings swing 70% if stock comp spikes on retention grants. FCF better proxy than net.

The short's story: Reddit is a 46% logged-in attention app that rents its audience from Google and sells its corpus to the companies trying to replace Google — a arbitrage that lasts only until the corpus is exhausted or Google stops paying.

8 — Final Words

Thesis: Reddit is the rare attention asset where 8 straight quarters >60% growth + 91% gross + first profit ($530m) + 61% Q2 beat still trades at a growth multiple that requires 20% CAGR to hold. At $161.70 you pay for 20% CAGR and get the corpus free — if Reddit Answers converts searchers to logged-in, ARPU gap to Meta closes, you get 24% CAGR and $195 fair. If Google Overviews cut logged-out 20% and mods revolt on ad load, you get 15% and $125.

Bull: DAUq 200m, US ARPU $14 (performance ads win), licensing $340m — rev $8.4bn 2030, $1,741m net (20.7% margin), rerates to 10× sales → $84bn EV → $426 / share. IRR ~28%.

Bear: DAUq 130m, licensing flat $150m, logged-out −15% on Overviews, ARPU $6 → rev $4.5bn, 25% EBITDA → $126 bear case (−22%). That's the arbitrage: upside is ARPU, downside is Google.
