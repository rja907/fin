1 — History: From Soviet Savings Bank to Super App

Kaspi's history matters because it explains the moat: a second-chance bank privatization + a product-obsessed founder + a country that leapfrogged from cash to QR.

1990s — The shell. The legal predecessor traces to Soviet-era savings banks consolidated as Bank Caspian in the 1990s, later Kaspi Bank. By the mid-2000s it was a mid-tier Kazakh bank: branch-heavy, deposit-funded, largely forgettable.

2007-2008 — The takeover. In 2006-2007 Vyacheslav Kim — a Kazakh entrepreneur who built the Planet of Electronics retail chain — bought into Kaspi Bank. In 2007 Kim partnered with Mikheil Lomtadze, a Georgian-born ex-Baring Vostok partner who had turned around Russian insurer Rosgosstrakh's tech. Baring Vostok (the PE firm behind Yandex, Ozon) bought ~50% and installed Lomtadze as CEO in 2007. The thesis was unconsensual: Kazakhstan had ~18m people, ~60% unbanked digitally, but mobile penetration was surging.

2008-2014 — Near-death and rebuild. The 2008 global crisis hit Kazakh banks brutally — BTA Bank defaulted, the tenge devalued 20% in 2009 and again ~25% in 2014. Kaspi survived by avoiding wholesale funding and FX mortgages, sticking to short-duration consumer loans and deposits. Lomtadze cut branches from 200+ to <100 and rebuilt around a single app. Kaspi Gold (debit + P2P) launched in 2016 with free P2P — Kazakhstan's Venmo moment. Growth exploded: from 0 to 5m users in ~18 months, because P2P was free and instant while Halyk and Forte charged.

2014-2019 — Marketplace. Payments engagement was the wedge. Once consumers had Kaspi Gold, Kaspi added m-Commerce (pay by QR in-store, 2017) then e-Commerce marketplace (2018) — an asset-light Amazon where Kaspi takes 5-12% without inventory (except e-Grocery 2023). Kaspi Pay for merchants (QR + POS) followed, with 600k+ merchants by 2024. GMV went from ~$1bn (2018) to ~$14bn (2024 avg-rate basis). Critically, Kaspi closed the loop: merchant sales → merchant deposits at Kaspi Bank → cheap funding for BNPL/consumer loans → more spending → more deposits.

2020-2024 — Listings. IPO on London Stock Exchange Oct 2020 (GDRs) at ~$33.50 ($6.5bn cap) was Kazakhstan's largest. Inclusion in MSCI Frontier. Nasdaq US listing via American Depositary Shares Jan 2024 (F-1, $92 ADS) — the stated goal: reach a larger investor base and use USD stock for M&A. Share count remarkably stable: ~199m ADS/share equivalents.

2025 — Hepsiburada. Jan 29, 2025 Kaspi announced $1.13bn for 65.4% of Turkish e-commerce leader Hepsiburada (Nasdaq: HEPS), closed Q2 2025 after regulatory approval. First non-Kazakhstan bet. FY2025 revenue was KZT 4,046bn ($8.00bn at ~505.4 avg) — +60% YoY (consolidated from Q2, incl. Hepsiburada) — ~25pp is Hepsiburada consolidation (first-party retail, lower margin). This is the fork: does Lomtadze replicate the Kazakh flywheel in an 85m-person, loss-making market?

Why history matters: Owner-operator culture (Lomtadze/Kim still own ~27%), paranoia from living through 2 devaluations, and a single-app doctrine — one app, one brand, one tech stack — are the moat's cultural source. Contrast with banks that built 4 apps.

2 — How the Business Actually Works

Kaspi is not a bank. It is a payments-led, marketplace-monetized, fintech-funded closed loop serving ~14m monthly active consumers (~70% of Kazakhstan's population) and ~660k active merchants in a country of ~20m.

Payments Platform (23% of revenue, ~38% of net income)

What it does: Kaspi Gold card (free P2P, bill pay), Kaspi QR (in-store), Kaspi Pay Super App for merchants (accept QR/other cards, B2B supplier pay, invoice + POS Register). 660k merchants, 14m+ consumers, 90%+ of transactions cashless by 2024 vs <20% in 2016.

How it makes $: Interchange/acquiring fee on TPV (Total Payment Value) plus interest on float (zero-interest current accounts). TPV ~$80bn (2024, KZT37.6 trillion at avg), Payments revenue $1.25bn ⇒ take ~1.5% blended. Very high margin ( Payments EBITDA margin ~70%+) because incremental transaction cost ≈ 0. Revenue KZT 587bn ⇒ $1.25bn USD (2024 avg). Growth 22% YoY USD.

Moat within moat: QR + Gold is habitual (30+ transactions / active consumer / month). B2B Payments and Kaspi Pay Business lock merchants: once suppliers are paid via Kaspi, deposits stay in-ecosystem.

Marketplace Platform (~28-32% of revenue, ~30% of income)

What it does: m-Commerce (QR in-store pickup), e-Commerce (3P marketplace + 1P e-Grocery since Feb 2023 + Kolesa auto classifieds), Kaspi Travel (flights/rail/holidays — fastest take). Kaspi handles delivery (Kaspi Express 50 warehouses), advertising (Kaspi Advertising — promoted listings), and 35% of merchants use POS Register.

How it makes $: Seller fee (take rate 8-12% → blended ~8.5% on GMV), delivery fee, ad fee, plus 1P retail revenue at cost (gross margin ~10% vs 3P fee ~100%). 1P mix rose to ~12% of Marketplace GMV — dilutes margin, but drives frequency (e-Grocery 2x/week vs e-Commerce 1x/month).

2024: GMV ~KZT ~6,600bn ⇒ ~$14.0bn USD (at 470 avg). Marketplace revenue ~KZT ~720bn ⇒ ~$1.53bn USD (est.; company splits not fully disclosed, derived from total 2,532bn - Payments 587bn - Fintech ~1,225bn). Take ~8.7%. Travel GMV +60% YoY, take ~4-6% (higher than e-Commerce's ~7%).

Fintech Platform (~45-50% of revenue, ~31% of net income but the funding engine)

What it does: Deposits (Kaspi Deposit — savings), Buy-Now-Pay-Later (0-24 months), consumer installment loans, merchant/micro-business finance. Average Net Loan Portfolio ~KZT 4,900bn ($10.4bn avg-rate). TFV (Total Finance Value — originations) ~KZT 10.3tn ($21.9bn) in 2024. Avg savings balances funding loans: 70%+ loan-to-deposit, duration <12 months.

How it makes $: Fintech yield ~ 26-30% (interest + fees) minus cost of funds 8-10% (deposits) and Risk Cost 4-6% (CoR). Net interest margin ~18-20%. 2024 Fintech revenue ~KZT 1,225bn ⇒ ~$2.61bn USD. Net income ~KZT 328bn ⇒ ~$698m. Yield compressing as BNPL (lower yield, lower risk) replaces cash loans.

Why it's the flywheel: Deposits are sticky because merchants *must* keep proceeds in Kaspi Pay to pay suppliers/staff for free. That cheap funding makes BNPL possible at 0% promo, which drives Marketplace GMV, which drives deposits. Underwriting uses Payments + Marketplace data (e.g., merchant turnover via Kaspi Pay → credit limit). First-payment default rate <2%, 90+ delinquency 3-4% — best in Kazakh banking.

The plumbing in one line: Consumer tops up Kaspi Gold (free) → pays merchant via QR → merchant sees instant settlement in Kaspi Pay → leaves balance (0% interest to merchant) → Kaspi lends it as BNPL back to consumer → consumer buys again on Marketplace → Kaspi takes take-rate twice (payments + marketplace) plus net interest. Loop count: avg consumer does 65 marketplace purchases / year + 360 payments / year.

4 — Competitive Dynamics & Moats

Moats (in order of durability)

1. Two-sided habit + single app

1 app (Kaspi.kz) holds payments, shop, travel, bank, post-purchase. Switching cost is not tech but habit: 65 purchases + 360 payments/year per active consumer → 425 interactions/year. Churn is ~5% because leaving means re-learning bill pay, losing free P2P to 14m peers. Halyk's app is bank-first; Kaspi is life-first. Design: 90% of support automated, NPS 80+.

2. Merchant lock-in via Kaspi Pay + deposits

661k merchants. Kaspi Pay is not just acquiring — it is their operating system: inventory, POS Register (mandatory fiscalization in Kazakhstan 2025), B2B supplier pay. Merchant deposits (current accounts) are the cheap funding that competitors cannot replicate without acquiring. Halyk has corporate deposits, but Kaspi's SME deposits are stickier (daily settlement). Result: Kaspi funds 70% of loans with 0-2% deposits vs peers at 8-12% wholesale.

3. Data-driven underwriting

Short-duration consumer loans (avg 11 months) + real-time data (merchant turnover, consumer payment regularity, Marketplace return rate) → loss rate 4-5% vs Kazakh peer 8-12%. First-payment default <2% (if you miss payment 1, you are cut off app — powerful incentive). This is a scale moat: more data → better risk → cheaper BNPL → more GMV → more data. LTV data advantage compounds.

4. Super App cost advantage (tech + logistics)

One tech stack for three platforms = tech cost ~3% revenue vs banks 8-12%. Kaspi Kartomats (4,600 lockers), 240 branches, 50 e-Grocery dark stores — capex-light vs Amazon/Ozon because Kazakhstan is dense (Almaty/Astana 35% of GDP). Delivery cost per order ~$1.50 vs $5+ in Turkey — hence Marketplace already profitable while Hepsiburada burns cash.

5 — Risks — What Would Make Me NOT Own It?

1. Single-country / KZT concentration — the permanent risk

~97% of revenue is Kazakhstan (pre-Hepsiburada). KZT devalued 50% vs USD in 2015-2016, 10% in 2023-2024 spot (avg 456 → spot 525). If KZT devalues 30% again (oil shock, Russia contagion), USD-reported revenue collapses 30% even if tenge revenue grows 32%. Hedging: Kaspi assets/liabilities matched in KZT, but USD investors bear FX. Payout ratio in KZT, dividend in USD suffers.

Mitigant: dollarization of Kazakh economy is ~40%, Kaspi has no FX mismatch (loans/deposits both KZT). Hepsiburada adds TRY diversification — but TRY is worse (70% deval 2021-2024).

2. Regulatory & rent extraction

Kazakhstan is not rule-of-law OECD. 2022 Jan unrest triggered windfall tax chatter; 2023-2024 government pushed mandatory POS fiscalization (helped Kaspi) but also discussed interchange caps (would cut Payments take 1.56% → 1.0%, -$450m revenue). BNPL regulation pending. Georgian Lomtadze holds outsized influence — political risk if relations shift. License is Kaspi Bank JSC — any banking misconduct hits all three platforms.

3. Fintech cycle & asset quality

Consumer lending in Kazakhstan is cyclical and short-term. If unemployment spikes, 90+ NPL could double from 3.6% to 7% — provision would cut net income ~$400m (2024 profit $2.25bn). Merchant/micro BNPL (17% TFV, fastest grower) is new vintage — no crisis history. Cost of risk 5.1% already elevated vs 2023 4.8%. Oversupply of deposits (if rates drop) would compress yield faster than funding cost.

4. Hepsiburada — the thesis breaker

$1.13bn for 65% of a Turkish loss-maker (HEPS lost ~$100m 2024, EBITDA margin -2%). Turkey is opposite of Kazakhstan: fragmented logistics, fierce competition (Trendyol ~55% share, Hepsiburada ~12%), inflation 60%+, TRY deval. If Kaspi exports its model and succeeds, TAM triples (20m → 105m). If it subsidizes losses for 3 years ($300m+), ROIC collapses from ~60% to ~30% and dividend coverage breaks. Q2 2025 first consolidated quarter will reveal: watch Hepsiburada contribution margin, not GMV.

The bear case in one sentence: You are paying a super-app multiple (see valuation ~12× 2024 IFRS net income avg-rate) for a bank that lives in one commodity currency, lends to the same 14m people it monetizes for shopping, and just levered culture onto a Turkish turnaround. The bull case is that this is exactly why it compounds — owner-operators with data and deposits that no competitor can replicate east of Poland.

8 — Final Words — Thesis Summary & What to Watch

Thesis in one paragraph: Kaspi is a 14m-MAU, 661k-merchant closed loop that funds BNPL with 0% merchant deposits, monetizes that loop twice (payments take + marketplace take + spread), and reinvests at 58% ROE with a 41% net margin. At 8.2× 2024 IFRS earnings (avg-rate USD), you are paying Halyk-bank multiple for Mercado Libre-like compounding — because market prices Kazakhstan single-country + KZT + Hepsiburada risk. If Lomtadze keeps credit disciplined and KZT devalues only 3-4% annually (in line with inflation differential), base-case DCF ~$249 offers >150% upside; even a punitive 15% WACC / 22% terminal margin still clears $159. The cheapness is real, but so is the concentration — this is a 10% position that can gap 30% on a tenge devaluation.

Bull case: KZ-only revenue CAGR 16% USD, margin holds 33%, Hepsiburada turns EBIT-positive 2027 by plugging Kaspi Pay/BNPL + ad engine — Kaspi becomes the "Alibaba of the Turkic corridor" (Kazakhstan 20m → Turkey 85m + Uzbekistan/Azerbaijan optionality). Rerates to 14× earnings ($165) + 6% yield. IRR ~24%.

Bear case: Hepsiburada burns $600m over 3 years, KZT spot drops to 650 (30% deval), CoR spikes to 8% in Kazakh consumer downturn — net 2027 $2.8bn not $3.8bn, multiple compresses to 6×. Downside to ~$55 ( -43% ). That's not a recession case — it is a currency + credit double.
