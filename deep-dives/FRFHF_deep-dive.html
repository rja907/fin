0 — TL;DR: What Fairfax actually is

Read this and you understand 80% of the thesis

Fairfax is not an insurer that invests — it is an investment firm funded by insurance float. Prem Watsa bought a tiny trucking insurer (Markel Financial) for $5m in 1985, renamed it Fairfax (“fair, friendly acquisitions”), and spent 40 years copying Berkshire Hathaway: (1) grow insurance premiums at ~95% combined ratio → keep float for free, (2) invest that float aggressively in bonds + concentrated equities/associates, (3) let book value per share do the talking.

1 — History: How a $5m trucking insurer became a $41B conglomerate

1985–2026: Watsa, Hamblin, and a very expensive education in hedging

1984–1987: The bet. Prem Watsa, an IIT Madras chemical engineer turned GW Asset Management / Confederation Life investor, co-founds Hamblin Watsa Investment Counsel (1984) with Tony Hamblin (his CIO at Confed). In 1985 they scrape together $5m (Robbert Hartog, Confed) to buy Markel Financial, a near-insolvent Ontario trucking insurer controlled by the Virginia Markel family. Float: $40m. Watsa’s insight, borrowed from Francis Chou and Buffett: buy an insurer for its float, run it conservatively, invest brilliantly. Renamed Fairfax in 1987 — “fair, friendly acquisitions.” Book $1.52/share.

1987–2000: Roll-up and reputation. Fairfax serially acquires distressed P&C: Federated, Commonwealth, Odyssey Re (1996, the U.S. reinsurance beachhead), Crum & Forster (1998), TIG runoff. Premiums explode but so does leverage. Fairfax becomes known for deep-value equity picks and macro hedges. Book compounds ~25% CAGR through 1998 — then the first near-death: reserves at TIG and C&F prove woeful, combined ratios spike >110%. Fairfax injects capital, learns that underwriting discipline matters more than cheap purchase price.

2003–2010: Redemption. Watsa hires better insurance operators (Andy Barnard), tightens reserving, and nails the 2008 crisis: Fairfax had bought CDS/deflation hedges that produce $2B+ gains while banks burn. Book jumps, Prem is anointed “Canada’s Buffett.” Share price peaks ~$415 CAD (pre-split equivalent).

2010–2020: The lost decade — and why it matters now. Fairfax keeps the deflation hedges on too long, shorts/hedges equities into a bull market (cost ~$1B+ in carry), and makes a concentrated bet on BlackBerry ($500m → $1B+). Book grows only ~4–7% CAGR; stock underperforms S&P by ~200pp. The lesson calcifies into two Watsa rules: no more equity hedges (removed Nov 2016, finished 2020) and own great businesses, don’t just buy cheap ones.

2021–2026: The snap-back. Three moves change the trajectory: (1) Shorten duration before the rate spike — Fairfax goes to ~1.5yr duration with T-bills, then locks 3–5yr at 4.5–5% as rates peak; interest & dividends triple from $530m (2021) to ~$2.2B (2025). (2) Monetize merchant-banking: sell RiverStone Europe, Toys “R” Us Canada, Resolute stub, Digit/ICICI Lombard partials. (3) Hard-market underwriting: gross premiums $13.8B (2017) → $32–33B (2024–25) at 92–93% combined — almost all organic. Book +34% in 2021, +16% in 2022 ex, +25% in 2023, +14.5% in 2024, +19% in 2025. The stock follows.

The 2010–2020 pain is why durations are short, hedges are gone, and total-return swaps on its own stock exist (1.76m shares, $664m notional) — a self-referential bet that book will grow.

2 — How Fairfax actually works

Two engines, one balance sheet — insurance funds investing, investing bails out insurance

Engine 1: Insurance & reinsurance — the float factory

Fairfax owns ~10 operating groups (all property & casualty, not life), run independently:

  • North American Insurance: Northbridge (Canada), Crum & Forster (US specialty), Zenith (workers comp)
  • Global (Re)Insurers: Odyssey Group (global re, largest), Allied World (Bermuda), Brit (Lloyd’s), Falcon, Polish Re
  • International: Fairfax Asia (Singapore, India via ICICI Lombard 8% + Digit 49%), South America (Argentina, Brazil, Chile), South Africa (Bryte)
  • Runoff: RiverStone (legacy U.S./UK) — now largely sold

Model: collect premiums today, pay claims later. The float (~$35–37B) is invested in the interim. If combined ratio <100%, float is paid to hold (benefit). Fairfax’s 8-yr average CR ~94%, and last 3 yrs ~92.7% — i.e., negative cost of float of ~5% (vs 10-yr U.S. Treasury ~3%). That is the moat.

PLUMBING: $33.3B gross → $26.3B net written (ceded to reinsurers) → $~25B net earned → × 92.7% CR = ~$1.8B underwriting profit (2024). That profit is before investment income. In a hard market, Fairfax also pushes rate +8–12% and keeps retention high.

Engine 2: Investing — Hamblin Watsa

Hamblin Watsa (HWIC), the original 1984 counsel, still manages ~all $75B. Allocation at Dec 31 2025 (Wade Burton, CIO):

  • $50B Fixed income — ~70% gov/IG, avg duration ~3–4yr, yield ~5.0%. This “supports reserves.” Every 100bp of yield = ~$500m pre-tax interest & dividends.
  • $24.9B Stocks + associates + LPs — mark-to-market common stocks (e.g., Eurobank, Stelco/Algoma, Quess, Fairfax India 43%, Grivalia Hospitality), equity-accounted associates (Eurobank ~33%, Poseidon/Atlas ~37%, Recipe, Thomas Cook India), and total-return swaps on 1.76m Fairfax shares.
  • Cash/Short-term $7B (10%) — dry powder for cat losses or dislocations.

Lumpy by design. 2023 net gains $2.0B, 2024 gains $1.1B, 2025 gains $3.05B (stocks $1.49B + swaps $0.84B). Plus interest & dividends $2.0–2.2B. That’s ~$4–5B of the $3.9–4.8B net earnings.

What each KPI tells you

  • Gross/Net premiums: Top-line scale. Fairfax doubled GPW 2017→2025 (+109%) almost organically. In soft markets it shrinks — discipline over volume.
  • Combined ratio: The insurance “gross margin.” 93% = 7¢ profit per $1 premium. Below 95% for 8 of last 9 yrs (ex-2017 hurricanes 99%). 92.7% in 2024 was a record.
  • Float: The liability that acts like equity. $20.4B (2017) → ~$37B (2025) = +$17B of zero-to-negative-cost funding, growing ~8% CAGR. Benefit of float 2023 was 5.5% (i.e., underwriting profit/float).
  • Interest & dividends: The annuity. $641m (2021) → $2.2B (2025) on the back of $50B at 5% — the single biggest reason book re-accelerated.
  • Net gains + BVPS: Gains are lumpy; BVPS smooths it. Look at BVPS 5-yr CAGR (~16–18%), not quarterly EPS.
Unit economics per $100 of net premium (2025A): Premium $100 → losses ~$63 + expenses ~$29.5 = combined $92.5 → UW profit $7.50. That $7.50 funds float which earns ~$8.40 of interest/dividends per $100 premium (float ≈1.4× premium). So insurance plus investing = ~$15.90 operating profit per $100 premium before marks.

5 — Moats, Competition & Risks

Why Fairfax can charge more — and what would make it stop

3 moat sources that actually persist

1) Diversified, decentralized underwriting. No single line >25% of premium. Odyssey, Allied, Brit, C&F, Northbridge each have separate management, culture, and reinsurance treaties. Hard-market pricing power comes from being everywhere — when Florida wind is soft, India motor is hard. Combined ~92–93% while peers (Travelers 96%, Chubb 93–94%, WR Berkley 93%) shows the breadth works.

2) Float scale + reserving discipline. $37B float at negative cost is a bigger, cheaper funding base than most reinsurers’ equity. Fairfax’s reserve redundancies (favorable PYD ~1–2pts most years) let it grow net premiums 73% since 2017 without leverage blowing out. Rating agencies (AM Best A) anchor on this.

3) HWIC’s “go-anywhere” mandate + permanence. Unlike a mutual fund, Fairfax can hold Eurobank through Greek crises (2013→2023), buy 1.76m shares via TRS, or sit in T-bills for 3 years — no redemptions. That permanence plus Prem’s two-generation network (India’s Fairbridge, Greece’s Eurolife, U.S. Stelco) is the investing edge.

Risks that break the thesis

  • Cat + reserve miss: $1B cat load is normal now; $2–3B clustered cats (Ian + Milton-type) could push CR to 98–100% and dent float benefit. A repeat of 1999–2001 adverse development would hit both UW and book.
  • Rate gives back: Hard market (+8–12% rate) is 4 yrs running. If pricing rolls -5% (inflation cools, capacity returns), premiums stall and combined drifts to 95–96% — still profitable but BV growth slows to low-teens.
  • Equity concentration: ~30% of equity is Eurobank/Atlas/Quess/Recipe/India — a Greek bank, a containership lessor, Indian outsourcing. Any one can be BlackBerry 2.0 if underwriting patience runs out.
  • Key man & TRS leverage: Prem is 75 (born 1950). TRS on 1.76m shares is ~$1.2B exposure — if FFH falls 30%, the mark is -$360m and hits net earnings immediately.

What would I need to believe to NOT own it? That the 2021–2025 duration trade was luck, not skill — and that the next rate cycle inverts the $50B bond book (duration 3yr × 150bp = ~$2.2B unrealized loss) while cats push combined to 99% simultaneously. Then book stalls, P/B compresses from 1.4× to 1.0×, and you earn ~5% not 15%.

What I think is mispriced

I think the market still prices Fairfax as a cyclical P&C (“hard market ends, book stalls”) and underweights the duration trade. $50B at 5% locked for 3–4 yrs is an annuity that didn’t exist in 2019 — it funds 1.7 points of ROE even if combined goes to 96%. Add $25B of equities at ~1.5× book (Eurobank/Stelco) and associates accounted below fair value, and 12% BVPS CAGR is the floor, not the ceiling, provided Prem doesn’t swing hard into a new BlackBerry. Sensitivity to watch: 50bp of rate — every -50bp on reinvestment = -$250m interest & divs = -1.2pt of BV growth. Watch the Q3’26 duration disclosure.

Stance: At 1.42× Dec book (1.31× H1), Fairfax is fairly priced for 12% and cheap for 14%+ — which requires combined <94% and equities adding ≥$800m/yr. I’d rather own the annuity (bonds) and get the equities for free than pay 1.85× for Chubb’s pure UW. Not advice.

8 — Final Words: own the book, not the quarter

What to watch (next 12 mo)

  • Combined in Q3’26 cat season — if Milton/Helene-like cats recur and Fairfax still posts <97%, the reserve culture is intact. If CR prints 99%+ without a mega-cat, something slipped.
  • Duration disclosure in Q3 Interim — is Wade extending at 4.2% or sitting short? The reinvestment on $6–7B maturing per year is the 2027 earnings call.
  • Eurobank/Atlas marks — Eurobank trades ~€2.60 vs Fairfax carry ~€1.40; unrealized gain is ~$1.5B pre-tax not in book until sold/remeasured. A sale would crystalize a book step like 2023’s Resolute exit.
  • Buyback cadence — if price dips <1.15× H1 book, expect $300–500m buyback. If not, the “buy below book” mantra is just talk.

What would make me not own it

I would not own Fairfax if I believed (1) the 92.7% CR is non-repeatable and the next 5 yrs average 97%+, (2) HWIC uses the current $7B cash to chase a single concentrated equity at >15% of portfolio (BlackBerry déjà vu), or (3) Prem’s succession is not real — no clear external CEO after Wade/Peter if Prem steps back. All three would show first in combined + reserve triangles, second in 13-D filings, third in the proxy — not in the stock price until it’s late.
