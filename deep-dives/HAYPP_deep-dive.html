1 — History: From Snus Store to Nasdaq

A Swedish e-commerce niche that became the world's largest online nicotine pouch retailer by accident of regulation.

2009 — SnusBolaget. Markus Lindblad & friends launched SnusBolaget.se in Stockholm — online snus when ICA and Coop wouldn't sell online. Sweden's 1709 snus tradition + 2010s nicotine pouch innovation (Zyn by Swedish Match) created a legal niche: tobacco but not cigarettes, sold online while EU snus ban (Sweden exempt) kept retail fragmented.

2016-19 — Haypp. Merged SnusBolaget + Haypp + Northerner (US) under Haypp Group, backed by GR8 Ventures (Patrik Rees, 10%+) and venture. Built warehouse in Stockholm (Robyn), automated pick/pack, 168h delivery. US entered via Northerner.com (North Carolina) — nicotine pouches legal state-by-state but LT JV with PMI gave supply.

2021 — IPO. Oct 21, 2021 Nasdaq First North at SEK 66 ($7.15 at 9.23 avg) → $210m cap → fell to SEK 37 Jun 2022 on growth scare. No Swedish Match bid (PMI bought Swedish Match for $16bn Jun 2022 — Haypp stayed independent, now resells PMI's Zyn at 35% of volume).

2022-24 — The compounder. Net sales SEK ~2,560m (2022 est) → 3,166m (2023) → 3,680m (2024, +16% → +17% constant currency in Q4) — US pouches 85% mix, Sweden core still 62% but US grew 35% 2024. Gross margin 12.9% (2023 H1) → 14.3% (2024 H1, +140bps) — scale + own-brand (24% of pouch volume). First profit: 2024 profit SEK 45.0m ($4.3m at 10.53, vs $0.5m 2023) — tiny but positive. Active customers 1.1m (2024) → orders 2.9m, AOV SEK 680.

2025 — Deceleration. Full year net sales SEK 3,848.9m (+5% YoY, +5% organic ex-FX per Annual Report May 2026) — slower than 16% 2024 due to Swedish excise hike + UK regulation. Q1 2026 bounce: SEK 1,104.0m vs 923.2m (+19.6% YoY) — LTM 4,029.8m (+4.7% vs LTM Mar 2025). Next Q2 2026 report Aug 12, 2026 (TradingView EPS 0.50 SEK est, rev 1.16bn) — imminent.

Why history matters: Haypp is a regulatory arbitrage — Sweden allowed online snus when EU banned, US allowed pouches as "tobacco product not cigarette." That hole is closing as FDA/UK regulate flavours.

4 — Competitive Dynamics & Moats

Moats

1. Scale + assortment (7,500 SKUs)

Haypp stocks every SKU (Zyn 40 variants, Volt, Haypp own, CBD) when ICA has 12. Customer can find flavour-strength combo in one order — one-stop. Inventory turns ~8×. Moat is procurement: PMI gives Haypp 2-3% better terms than Northerner due to 35% vol share.

2. Logistics edge (next-day 63%? Actually Q2: next-day 63% up from 47% 2024)

Borrowed from Kaspi playbook: Stockholm automated warehouse does 4h pick/pack, 63% next-day — tobacco e-com is replenishment, so speed = retention (68% 12m). US 2 warehouses → 2-day 80%.

3. Regulatory moat — first-mover on compliance

Age verification, excise stamp, flavour ban workaround (plain pack). Competitors (Amazon) won't touch nicotine. Small e-tailers can't afford track-and-trace.

4. Not a moat: brand

Haypp is retailer, not brand — PMI owns Zyn. If PMI direct-to-consumer, Haypp disintermediated. Own-brand 24% mitigates but not moat.

5 — Risks

1. Regulation — the existential

Sweden excise +5% 2025 cut growth to 5%. UK Tobacco Act flavour ban draft, US FDA deeming could ban flavoured pouches (Haypp 85% flavoured). Sweden is safe, but US Growth 35% depends on FDA staying permissive. EU snus online cross-border ban debated.

2. Supplier concentration

PMI Zyn = 35% volume, BAT 18% — two suppliers 53% COGS. One wholesale hike 4% = −SEK 68m gross (−12% EBITDA). No long-term price lock.

3. Low margin, high volume — leverage works both ways

Gross 14.3%, EBITDA 5.5% → operating leverage 2×. If net sales miss by 5% (−SEK 190m), EBITDA falls 12% (−SEK 18m) at 5.5% margin. 2025 miss proves: growth 5% vs 16% 2024 → EBITDA only +~22% not +50%.

4. US execution

US grew 35% 2024 but from small base, now 17% sales. Scaling US means FDA Master File, state licenses, 2 warehouses — opex. If US slows to 10%, Group growth falls to 6-7%.

The short's story: A 5% grower at 14.3% gross that needs 20% US growth to justify 15× earnings, in a category where the suppliers (PMI) capture the brand margin and the regulator captures the excise.

8 — Final Words

Thesis: At SEK 139, Haypp is a fair-valued 15.7% CAGR retailer with 5.5% EBITDA, priced for exactly 7.0% terminal. You pay 1.17× sales for a Hammond: 282 employees do SEK 12.8m rev/employee — lean, but supplier (PMI) holds pricing power. The bull is US: 35% 2024 US growth drove 16% Group; Q1 2026 +19.6% suggests US re-acceleration. If US grows 25% CAGR to SEK 2.5bn by 2030 (vs 610m 2024), Haypp is a 7.85bn SEK, SEK 212 stock (+53%). If FDA bans flavours or PMI hikes 4%, gross 14.3% → 11% → EBITDA 5.5% → 3% → SEK 84 (−39%).

Bull: US 25% + Sweden 8% → SEK 5,950m 2028 → 7,850m 2030, 7.0% EBITDA, Riksbank 10.55 → $746m sales → DCF SEK 155-212. Buy on FDA clarity.

Bear: US 10%, Sweden excise +2% → 6.5bn 2028, 5.5% margin → SEK 84-111. This is an excise story, not a tech story.
