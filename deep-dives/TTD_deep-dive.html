1 — History: From Ad-Network Salesman to Kokai

Jeff Green built TTD because he saw the buy side would be independent or it wouldn't exist.

2009 — Founding. Jeff Green (Microsoft AdECN, AdBrite) and Dave Pickles (ex-Microsoft) founded TTD in Ventura, California, after selling Green add-on to Microsoft. Thesis: RTB would move branding dollars from TV IOs to programmatic pipes, but agencies would need an objective buyer — not Google who also sells the inventory.

2010-16 — Bootstrapped growth. Self-funded, profitable from year 2. Raised only $120m total. Built Bidder (single global bidder, 13m QPS by 2016). IPO Sep 2016 at $18 ($1.1bn cap) — 5× oversubscribed, first ad-tech IPO to stick.

2017-21 — CTV breakout. The pivot that compounded: CTV spend went from ~5% of platform (2018) to >40% by 2023. Roku, Hulu, Disney embraced TTD because it was not competing for their viewers. Revenue CAGR 2016-21: 39% ($203m → $1,196m). Gross margin held 76-78% — software, not media.

2021-24 — The Kokai & identity war. Apple ATT (2021) kneecapped mobile IDs; third-party cookie deprecation loomed. TTD launched UID2 (hashed email, open-source, now EUID across 15+ countries) and OpenPath (direct publisher pipes to bypass SSP take). In Jun 2023, TTD unveiled Kokai — AI-based media-buying platform (KoAI decisioning). By Q3 2024, 70% of spend was flowing via Kokai features; CTV share >48%.

2025 — Peak. FY2025 revenue $2.896bn (+18.5% YoY, XBRL 10-K), adj. EBITDA $1.15bn (40% margin), FCF $680m. Stock peaked at $139.51 Dec 4, 2024 (macro + AI hype). Then the turn: Q4 2024 already missed whisper, Kokai migration disrupted workflows; clients complained about UI change.

2026 — The crack. Q1 2026 (May 8): revenue $689m (+11.8% YoY, 1.4% beat) but guide Q2 ≥$750m vs $772m est — stock −15%. Q2 2026 (Aug 6): revenue $715m (+3.0% YoY) vs $752m est (−4.9% miss), adj. EBITDA $241m (33.7% margin, −190bps vs 40% target) vs $262m est, EPS $0.34 vs $0.40; Q3 guide $650m midpoint vs $805m est (−19% gap) — stock −21.9% to $13.80 Aug 7. Market cap fell from $65bn to $6.5bn (−85% YoY). Citi downgrade Aug 7: "further tumble possible." Jeff Green on call: "macro + Kokai transition + retail media share loss."

Why history matters: TTD's culture is product-obsessive and anti-wallet-garden — exactly the moat, and exactly why Kokai's forced migration (a Bezos-style rebuild) hurt near-term trust.

2 — How the Business Actually Works

TTD is not an ad network. It is a demand-side platform (DSP) that media buyers (trading desks at WPP, Omnicom, Nike) use to buy impressions across the open internet — CTV, display, audio, out-of-home — but not inside Google Search/YouTube, Meta, or Amazon walled gardens (35% of ad spend TTD cannot touch).

Revenue model — a toll, not media

How it charges: Percentage of media spend placed through platform. If Nike spends $100 on Hulu via TTD, TTD keeps ~$19-20 (take rate), passes $80 to publisher/SSP. Revenue is net (principal vs agent) — TTD never says "gross spend," but analysts estimate 2024 gross spend ~$12.1bn → revenue $2.445bn = 20.2% take.

Margin profile: Gross margin 80-82% (2024: 82%; 2023: 81%) because incremental bid costs ~0 (AWS). Operating costs are people (headcount ~3,700) + data centers + SBC. EBITDA margin target ≥40% at maturity — disciplined.

Mix 2025: CTV ~45% of spend (+18% YoY), display ~25%, audio ~12%, retail media data offsite ~8% (Walmart, Kroger). US ~88% of revenue; international ~12% (EU UID2 rollout).

Three platform bets

1. Kokai (2023-26): Rebuilt UI around KoAI — budget forecasting, audience creation, bid optimization. TTD claims 24% lower CPA for Kokai users. But migration = "operating system upgrade while flying" — Q1-Q2 2026 churn/pause as traders relearn.

2. UID2 / EUID: Open identity: hashed email + consent. Adopted by ~80% of US premium publishers; Disney, NBCU, Paramount trade on it. Antidote to cookie death. Risk: Google Privacy Sandbox/Topics, regulatory scrutiny.

3. OpenPath + Sincera + retail media: OpenPath (2022) is direct pipe to publishers (cuts SSP 10-15% fee) — TTD benefits indirectly via better win rates. Retail media (offsite): Walmart Connect uses TTD to let P&G target Walmart shoppers on CTV — high growth, but Amazon owns 70% of retail media and won't use TTD.

Why the toll holds: Advertisers care about objectivity. Google DV360 also sells Google-owned YouTube inventory — conflict. TTD touches no media, so agencies trust it won't arbitrage them. That trust is the moat, not tech.

4 — Competitive Dynamics & Moats

Moats (in order of durability)

1. Objectivity + agency trust

WPP, Omnicom, IPG trust TTD because it doesn't own media. Google DV360 prioritizes YouTube; Amazon DSP prioritizes Amazon. TTD's pitch: "We buy only what's best for ROAS, not our inventory." That trust + $12bn spend scale = data moat. Durability: medium — survives Kokai wobble but not permanent retail media defection.

2. CTV & retail media data pipes

CTX: Roku, Disney, Hulu opened logs to TTD via UID2 — TTD knows who saw what CTV ad and bought what at Walmart (offsite). OpenPath gives direct bidstream (latency ↓ 90ms, win rate +15%). Retail media offsite is 8% of spend but 30% of growth — Walmart/Kroger chose TTD over Criteo because TTD can reach CTV, not just on-site. Moat: network effect (more spend → more logs → better KoAI), but fragile to Amazon closing loop.

3. Kokai / KoAI decisioning

Claims: 24% lower CPA, 20% less impressions for same outcome. If true, lock-in: campaign settings become model weights. Short-term, migration cost (retraining traders) hurt; long-term, AI personalization could raise take rate. Risk: if benefit is <10%, clients arbitrage across DSPs (Magnite, Yahoo).

4. Scale on open internet (13m QPS, supercomputing)

TTD bids on <1m impressions/sec globally, ML evaluates 300+ features/bid. CapEx-light but infra scale (AWS) is barrier vs subscale DSPs. Not a moat vs Google/Amazon who have 10× infra.

5 — Risks — What Would Make Me NOT Own It?

1. Walled-garden take-all

35% of digital spend is inside Google/Meta/Amazon walled gardens — TTD's SAM excludes it. If retail media (Walmart, Target) decide to go onsite-only and not let TTD extend reach to CTV offsite, TTD's growth engine stalls. Amazon already keeps 70% of retail media spend internal. TTD's offsite pitch weakens if cookies/UID2 adoption stalls.

2. Kokai migration — self-inflicted disruption

Kokai required traders to rebuild audiences, reports, curations. Q1-Q2 2026 shows clients paused: Q2 revenue missed own guide ($750m → $715m) and Q3 guide collapsed to $650m (-12% YoY). If Kokai's AI benefit doesn't materialize in 2 quarters, churn becomes permanent. History: TTD's prior Solimar upgrade (2021) caused 1-quarter wobble; this is 3× worse.

3. Take-rate compression & SBC dilution

Take has drifted 22% (2016) → 20% (2024). At scale, agencies negotiate: WPP can extract 18% or move to self-serve. If take slips to 17%, revenue falls 15% with no cost relief. SBC is 21-26% revenue — GAAP earnings perpetually ~50% below adj. EBITDA. At $13.80, SBC dilution overwhelms FCF.

4. CTV monetization maturity

CTV was 45% of spend; growth drove multiple. If CTV CPMs flatten as inventory oversupply hits (streamers all ad-tier), TTD's win rate gains don't translate to revenue. 2025 CTV already grew only 18% vs 25%+ prior — early sign.

The bear case in one sentence: You pay 15× adj. EBITDA for a DSP whose Kokai rebuild coincided with Amazon/retail media enclosing budgets and agencies discovering take can be arbitraged — so growth stalls to LSD while SBC still dilutes 5% annually.
