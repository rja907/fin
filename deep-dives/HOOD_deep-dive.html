1 — History: Free Trades That Cost $0 Until They Didn't

Two Stanford grads made trading free — then the bill came due on Jan 28, 2021.

2013 — Stanford roommates. Vlad Tenev (Stanford Math) & Baiju Bhatt (physics) left high-frequency firm in NYC, moved to Palo Alto, app that offered $0 commissions when Schwab charged $7. Waitlist 1m. Sequoia $13m seed. The secret was PFOF: sell order flow to Citadel/Virtu who pay $0.0017/share for the right to fill it — legal since 1980s, hidden from customers.

2018-20 — Crypto + cash. Robinhood Crypto Feb 2018 (no commission, spread), Cash Management (3% APY via partner banks). Funded customers 5m (2018) → 12.5m (2020). Revenue $278m (2019) → $959m (2020) — but 75% was PFOF, 17% was Robinhood Financial's net interest on cash sweeps. Business exploded in COVID retail boom: 2020 revenue ~$1.36bn? Actually 2021 $1.815bn (meme peak), then collapsed to $1.358bn 2022 when crypto winter + rate hikes crushed volumes (−38%). Net loss −$1.028bn 2022.

Jan 2021 — The reckoning. GameStop 159% short squeeze; NSCC demanded $3.7bn collateral overnight; Robinhood restricted buying — congressional hearing, "Roaring Kitty" meme. Reputation cratered, but also proved need for capital: raised $3.4bn overnight from Ribbit. Lesson: leverage needs deposits.

2021 — IPO & hangover. IPO Jul 29, 2021 at $38 ($32bn cap), fell to $10 by Jun 2022. Bhatt stepped back to CTO, Tenev stayed CEO. Turnaround: cut 23% staff, sunset Building products (Snacks), focused on net interest as rates rose.

2022-24 — The rate tailwind. Fed hiked to 5.25%: Robinhood's cash sweeps ($20bn+) + margin loans ($3bn) → net interest went from $269m (2022) → $919m (2023) → ~$1.1bn (2024, ~37% rev). Gold (subscription, $5/mo) launched premium 3-5% APY + IRA match 1-3% → Gold subs 1.7m (2023) → 2.6m (2024, +53%). Crypto rebounded: Trump 2024 win → BTC +70% → Robinhood crypto notional $82bn Q1 2026 (+16% YoY). Revenue $1.865bn (2023) → $2.951bn (2024, +58.2%) — first GAAP profit since IPO: +$1.411bn (includes $595m tax benefit & SBC reversal, adj. EBITDA ~$1.0bn).

2025-26 — The compounder? 2025 revenue $4.473bn (+51.6%), net +$1.883bn (+33% vs 2024 adj.) — beat as rates stayed high and options volumes +38% to 659m contracts, MAU 13m (down 12.8% but ARPU up). Acquired Bitstamp (Jun 2025, $200m) for crypto, + Rothera (Roth IRA?) + WonderFi Q2 2026. Q2 2026 (Jul 29): revenue $1.308bn (+32.3% YoY) vs $1.26bn est (+3.5% beat), net $386m (+105%), EPS $0.62, crypto volume $28bn (+32% QoQ) but crypto transaction revenue fell 38% YoY (spread compression) — total transaction still grew because options + equities offset. Net deposits $21.7bn quarter (28% ann.), 12m $75.7bn (+27% vs $282bn AUC at Q2 2025) — the flywheel: deposits → AUC → net interest → Gold.

Why history matters: Robinhood survived by turning free trades into a deposit franchise — exactly what banks do, but without a charter. The PFOF debate (SEC Chair Gensler threatened ban) never killed it; rates did the monetizing.

1. Transaction-based (PFOF + crypto) — ~55% rev, volatile

Equities PFOF: $0.0010-0.0017/share from Citadel/Virtu. Zero commission → Robinhood gets paid to route your market order. Revenue = volume × take. Q1 2026 options 659m contracts +38% → transaction $600m+.

Options: 2× equities PFOF per contract; 2025 options + equities drove 40% transaction growth.

Crypto: spread (0.5-1.5%) + Bitstamp exchange volume ($82bn Q1 notional +16%). Q2 crypto revenue fell 38% YoY despite volume +32% — spread compression as competition (Coinbase 1% vs Robinhood 0.6%). Crypto is 15-20% transaction.

2. Net interest — ~32% rev, rate-sensitive

Float: Customer uninvested cash swept to partner banks (4.25% Fed funds → Robinhood keeps 3.5% = net interest). AUC $282bn (Q2 2025 base) + $75.7bn deposits LTM → cash $20-25bn. Margin securities lending + stock loan. 2023 net interest $919m (49% rev) → 2025 ~$1,300m (29% rev) as rev diversifies. Falls 30bps per Fed cut (~$60m/25bps).

3. Other (Gold + retirement + card) — ~13% rev, recurring

Gold ($9.99/mo or $5): 4.8m subs (+39% YoY, +1.4m) → $57.6m ARR at $12/mo avg but also drives 2× ARPU: Gold ARPU $400+ vs non-Gold $120. Retirement IRA: 3% match (up to 1% after) → $8bn IRA AUC,sticky. Credit card: 3% cash back, $10/mo waitlist 2m. Other revenue Q2 ~$170m (13%). ARPU $187 LTM — vs Schwab $380, but Schwab has $8tn AUC.

The flywheel: Net deposits $21.7bn Q2 (28% ann.) → AUC → cash sweep → net interest → Gold subs → higher ARPU ($187 vs $150 non-Gold) → more deposits. Crypto/options are the acquisition hook; net interest + Gold is the annuity.

4 — Competitive Dynamics & Moats

Moats

1. Mobile UX + brand with Gen Z

~35% funded customers are 18-34. No minimums, fractional, instant deposit. Schwab/E*Trade have retirement assets, not mobile habit. NPS 60+. Durability: medium — design can be copied, but habit (options chains saved) sticks.

2. Manufacturing-cost advantage (no branches)

$0 branch vs Schwab 400 branches → cost/income 60% vs Schwab 55% but on $187 ARPU vs $380. If ARPU triples to $350, leverage explodes. Network effect: more AUC → more sec lending → better Gold rate → more net deposits → more AUC. 27% deposit growth is the moat in action.

3. Crypto regulatory capture

Bitstamp acquisition (EU license) + crypto revenue double in Q2 — only US retail broker with full crypto stack (Coinbase is exchange, not broker). Tokenization thesis (Huffman 2024): stock tokens on Robinhood chain.

4. Not a moat: PFOF

SEC Gensler threatened ban 2021-23, now tabled. PFOF take $0.0017/share is rent, not IP. If banned, revenue −20%. But status quo: PFOF must be "best execution" — Robinhood's price improvement $1.4bn LTM proves it.

5 — Risks

1. Crypto + options volume leverage

52% rev is transaction — exactly what collapsed −38% 2022. Crypto volumes +32% Q2 but rev −38% (spread down) shows paradox: volume can rise while take falls. 2025 51% rev growth was options +38% + crypto +16% — if retail goes risk-off, trans −30% → total −15% even with net interest flat.

2. Rates — the other half

32% rev is net interest — +30bps per cut = −$60m. Fed cut 75bps in 2026 priced? Hood guides OpEx $2.68bn but net interest already falling 1.8% → 1.5% if 100bps cut → $85m hit. The 2023-24 tailwind becomes headwind.

3. Regulatory (PFOF, prediction markets)

PFOF ban still on SEC shelf; prediction markets (Kalshi) could be deemed swaps. Robinhood launched Trump Accounts joint venture $100m (2026) — political risk.

4. SBC / dilution re-acceleration

2022 SBC ~$600m 44% rev, now 14% — disciplined. But WonderFi/Rothera deal + Trump Accounts adds $100m. Buyback $250m Q1 offset SBC, but authorization $1.5bn — if stock falls, buybacks become defensive not offensive.

The short story: A 28% deposit grower monetizing at $187 ARPU with 52% transaction mix is a levered retail volume bet that looks like a fintech compounder only when rates and crypto are both up — they haven't been both up for 18 months pre-2025.

8 — Final Words

Thesis: At $93, Robinhood is priced as a 26% CAGR story with 48% EBITDA — essentially Gold + deposit flywheel plus crypto kicker. Q2 proved net deposits 27% with 24% ARPU is real, and expense guide cut to $2.68bn proves discipline. But 52% rev is still transaction — a bear market cuts rev 30% while OpEx $2.68bn is sticky. The 2024-25 rerating (58%→51% growth) already harvested 3y of PFOF; from here you pay 18× sales for 21% forward growth — classic growth trap unless deposits stay 27% through a downturn.

Bull: Deposits 27% → AUC $520bn 2028, Gold 9m, ARPU $260, transaction still 55% but on larger base → rev $12.8bn 2030, $4.8bn net (37.5%), rerates to 15× → $192bn EV → $210 / share (+125%).

Bear: Crypto volumes −40%, options −20%, deposits 12% (like Schwab), net interest −$85m on cuts → rev $8bn, EBITDA 35% → $48 bear case (−49%).
